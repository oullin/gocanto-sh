title: Growth requires movement

description: "A brief reflection on leaving familiar routines, trying unfamiliar work, and treating movement as a condition for personal and professional growth."
tags: [leadership, growth, career]
---

Staying with familiar work has an obvious benefit: competence compounds. It also has a cost
when familiarity becomes the only reason to remain.

Movement can mean changing a role, learning a language, working with people whose assumptions
differ from mine, or taking on a problem for which I do not already have a method. None of
those choices guarantees growth. They create the conditions in which I have to pay attention
again.

The difficult part is distinguishing discomfort from damage. New work is often tiring
because I am learning. An environment that consistently removes agency, ignores
contributions, or makes good work impossible is tiring for a different reason. Persistence
helps with the first. Leaving may be the responsible answer to the second.

I try to ask two questions:

- Am I still learning something that matters to me?
- Is the cost of staying producing skill and perspective, or only inertia?

The answers change over time. A decision that was right two years ago can become a habit that
no longer serves the same purpose.

Movement is not a demand to chase novelty. It is a willingness to notice when the current
position has stopped teaching me, then take a deliberate step rather than waiting for the
decision to be made on my behalf.
